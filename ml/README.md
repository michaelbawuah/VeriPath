# Annual observed-crash frequency research

This pipeline forecasts strongly assigned police injury-crash counts on fixed road segments. It does not estimate a traveler's collision probability or a causal safety benefit.

Use the data downloader, then compile `engine/road_matcher.cpp` with `g++ -std=c++23 -O2`, and run `python ml/train_frequency.py --data PATH --matcher ABSOLUTE_BINARY --output public/model`. Requirements are listed beside this file.

2021 supplies lag history; 2022–2023 are training; 2024 selects regularization and the strongest baseline; 2025 was the experiment’s temporal audit and has now been examined repeatedly. It is not a pristine future validation set. A fixed 2 km spatial-block holdout checks coefficient transfer with local past history. Its regularization is selected using only non-held-out blocks; it is not a cold-start test. Training includes zero-crash eligible ground-level road segments. All road geometries remain matching competitors; quarantined geometries cannot receive strong assignments. Target-year labels never enter features. The historical experiment refit on 2022–2025 to forecast 2026. The research release gate requires a positive deviance gain over the preselected baseline, a positive lower bound from a paired 1,000-replicate geographic-block bootstrap, aggregate observed/predicted ratio 0.8–1.2, and observed/predicted ratios 0.5–2 for calibration deciles with at least 30 observed reports. These are prototype quality gates chosen during development, not a preregistered clinical or safety test. This gate alone does not validate route safety or prediction intervals.

Features are geometric segment length, previous-year count, and mean prior-three-year count (available preceding history in early training years). Models use no outcomes of unobserved future crashes, traffic volume, current posted speed or current lane design. The current LION roadbed geometry is a retrospective assignment reference, so historical redesign and segment changes remain a limitation.

A strong road assignment requires ground-level geometry, distance at most 20 m, and separation from a different SegmentID: 6 m with an agreeing street name, 12 m without one. Candidates are considered up to 35 m. A nearby competing non-ground or quarantined segment blocks a strong assignment. Street aliases are retained. These are transparent rules rather than calibrated match probabilities. The historical experiment excluded ambiguous segment assignments and elevated/tunnel geometry; the new intersection audit is a separate target definition and does not modify those historical model results. The validation report publishes exclusion counts, hashes, model coefficients, baseline metrics and calibration bins. A low-frequency forecast is not proof a route is safer.

The initial experiment remains withheld for every mode. Pedestrian deviance improves only marginally; its highest prediction decile underestimates observed reports by more than a factor of two. Most crash records are not strongly assigned. A zero target means zero eligible assigned reports, not evidence of no collisions.

## Matching coverage and independent review

`python ml/audit_matching.py --data PATH --matcher ABSOLUTE_BINARY --output public/quality` runs the C++23 `--intersections` audit over the frozen 2021–2025 injury/fatal snapshot. The legacy matcher mode remains available for reproducing the historical annual segment experiment. Its source state is GitHub commit `fac4ab0ae1e4f3c33afa11e890e03ee1ff9b874f`; current model artifacts retain the original experiment hashes. No forecast was retrained with the new node associations.

The v2 coverage audit associates reports either with street segments or named shared-node context; categories are mutually exclusive. Missing coordinates and outside-bounds records stay in the overall denominator. `off_street_name` means nearest cross street; `cross_street_name` contains address text. Neither nearest-cross naming nor line crossings prove intersection involvement.

The public quality page displays coverage and **Not evaluated** accuracy. A deterministic sample selects 200 unique cases within borough × assignment-status strata, with a minimum of two per stratum. A separate 60-case challenge sample contains node/ambiguous reports; it does not guarantee every difficult topology. Sampling probabilities are recorded in `ml/audit/sampling.json`; evaluator predictions are in `ml/audit/predictions.json`. The reviewer page receives neither. Its source-only packet preserves original road levels, shared node IDs and source names. This is presentation blinding, not an access-control guarantee: reviewers must avoid evaluator files and the public repository during independent review.

Have two independent reviewers export decisions from `/review`, without seeing each other's labels. If they disagree, a third reviewer supplies a separate export as adjudication after reading their rationales. Matching identity compares association type and sorted acceptable IDs. Unresolved cases and one-review cases remain pending. Reviewers can record ambiguous, insufficient-location and outside-scope decisions; the evaluator validates all candidate IDs and frozen hashes.

Run `python ml/evaluate_reviews.py --review REVIEW_A.json --review REVIEW_B.json --adjudication ADJUDICATION.json --output RESULT.json`. Omit `--adjudication` if there are no resolved disagreements. This evaluates population-weighted segment/node precision conditional on resolvable locations, supported-association recall, a separate unsupported-acceptance rate, and out-of-scope acceptances. The CLI recomputes reviewer-case, sampling and prediction hashes before evaluating. Partial review ratios are descriptive reviewed-subset statistics; population estimates are withheld until the entire representative packet is finalized. Challenge results remain unweighted and separate. No confidence interval or model release decision follows from these ratios alone. Human agreement is not verified physical ground truth. Synthetic unit fixtures are never review labels.

Development and final matching reviews must be partitioned by node/local spatial block before tuning; this initial packet is an audit, not a hidden final benchmark. Independent review, ambiguity reasons, local validation and exposure data remain required before a safer-route claim.

## Future forecasting protocol

`evaluation-protocol.json` records the proposed future protocol. Complete 2026 can become a previously unseen temporal test after reporting maturity; because it is already October 2026, this is not a wholly prospective experiment. A prospective annual experiment must freeze the pipeline before 2027 begins. Both require immutable prediction/target manifests, predeclared baselines (including the previously winning smoothed-history baseline) and per-mode calibration gates. Sparse calibration bins are pooled by the prescribed rule; insufficient calibration evidence withholds release. Forecast release remains disabled; no 2026 target outcomes were downloaded for this milestone.
