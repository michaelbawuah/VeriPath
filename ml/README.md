# Annual observed-crash frequency research

This pipeline forecasts strongly assigned police injury-crash counts on fixed road segments. It does not estimate a traveler's collision probability or a causal safety benefit.

Use the data downloader, then compile `engine/road_matcher.cpp` with `g++ -std=c++23 -O2`, and run `python ml/train_frequency.py --data PATH --matcher ABSOLUTE_BINARY --output public/model`. Requirements are listed beside this file.

2021 supplies lag history; 2022–2023 are training; 2024 selects regularization and the strongest baseline; 2025 is the final temporal test. A fixed 2 km spatial-block holdout checks coefficient transfer with local past history. Its regularization is selected using only non-held-out blocks; it is not a cold-start test. Training includes zero-crash eligible ground-level road segments. All road geometries remain matching competitors; quarantined geometries cannot receive strong assignments. Target-year labels never enter features. After evaluation, deployment refits on 2022–2025 to forecast 2026. The research release gate requires a positive deviance gain over the preselected baseline, a positive lower bound from a paired 1,000-replicate geographic-block bootstrap, aggregate observed/predicted ratio 0.8–1.2, and observed/predicted ratios 0.5–2 for calibration deciles with at least 30 observed reports. These are prototype quality gates chosen during development, not a preregistered clinical or safety test. This gate alone does not validate route safety or prediction intervals.

Features are geometric segment length, previous-year count, and mean prior-three-year count (available preceding history in early training years). Models use no outcomes of unobserved future crashes, traffic volume, current posted speed or current lane design. The current LION roadbed geometry is a retrospective assignment reference, so historical redesign and segment changes remain a limitation.

A strong road assignment requires ground-level geometry, distance at most 20 m, and separation from a different SegmentID: 6 m with an agreeing street name, 12 m without one. Candidates are considered up to 35 m. A nearby competing non-ground or quarantined segment blocks a strong assignment. Street aliases are retained. These are transparent rules rather than calibrated match probabilities. Ambiguous intersections and elevated/tunnel geometry are excluded. The validation report publishes exclusion counts, hashes, model coefficients, baseline metrics and calibration bins. A low-frequency forecast is not proof a route is safer.

The initial experiment remains withheld for every mode. Pedestrian deviance improves only marginally; its highest prediction decile underestimates observed reports by more than a factor of two. Most crash records are not strongly assigned. A zero target means zero eligible assigned reports, not evidence of no collisions.
